Your role

Walk beside them

Thank you for serving. Over 24 hours, young creatives will dream, build, and offer what they make back to God. Your job is not to build their project for them. It is to walk beside them, ask good questions, and help them see what God is doing in them and through them.

A coach and a companion

During the sprint, you're a coach and companion, not a project manager. At presentations you'll also judge, so see the Judging tab.

  • 01
    Come alongside

    Sit with the team, listen first, and let them lead.

  • 02
    Ask, don't tell

    A good question teaches more than a quick fix.

  • 03
    Protect their ownership

    The idea, the work, and the credit belong to the participants.

  • 04
    Point to Jesus

    Notice and name where God shows up in their ideas, struggles, and breakthroughs.

  • 05
    Care for the whole person

    Watch for tiredness, discouragement, and anyone left out of the team.

Do and don't

Do

  • Introduce yourself to every team early on Discord
  • Check in at least once every few hours
  • Celebrate small wins out loud
  • Encourage quieter members to share
  • Pray with teams when they are stuck or tired
  • Tell the organizers if something concerns you

Don't

  • Take over their screen or do the work for them
  • Compare teams or rank ideas privately
  • Overload them with your own opinions
  • Disappear from the server for long stretches
  • Promise things on behalf of the organizers
  • Let anyone work through the night with no rest

Our persona

Everyone makes for Nonoy

Every team starts from the same person. The rest of his story belongs to each team, so every team's Nonoy will be a little different, and so will what they make for him.

N

Nonoy, 19

curious about Jesus
Studies
College freshman in Iloilo. His main goal this year is not to fail his classes.
Home
Grew up in the city and lives with his family in Jaro.
Free time
Hangs out with friends and has time to browse social media.
Faith
Grew up in a traditional Filipino family. Curious about God and about Jesus, and willing to talk about what he believes.

How to use Nonoy

  • Ask each team to describe their version of Nonoy, and help them keep him specific and real.
  • Ask "How would Nonoy see this?" whenever an idea drifts or the scope keeps growing.
  • Check that what a team makes would truly speak to Nonoy, whether it's a content strategy, a film or reel, or a game.

The team ideation questions

Each team fills these in on the Nonoy page. One teammate answers for the team, and the answers stay on that phone only, so ask to see them early. They're your best starting point.

PartQuestions teams answer
01 · His world online
  • Which apps does he use most?
  • What makes him stop scrolling?
  • Who does he follow and trust online?
  • When is he online?
02 · His heart this Christmas
  • What does Christmas look like for Nonoy?
  • What is he feeling or struggling with?
  • What does he hope for, or quietly long for?
  • What does he think about Jesus, church, and Christians, and why?
03 · The gap
  • What keeps him from hearing or understanding the gospel?
  • What would make him actually listen, or share it with a friend?
04 · In his own wordsSomething Nonoy might actually say or post. English, Hiligaynon, or Taglish all work.
05 · Your idea for him"Nonoy needs… through…", then: How might we help Nonoy, a curious 19-year-old in Jaro, find [what he needs] through [your format] this Christmas?

Teams are reminded to pick real details, be specific ("lonely after his barkada moved away" beats "sad sometimes"), and stay curious: ask why at least twice.

How to use their answers

  • Read the answers first, then ask follow-up questions instead of giving your own ideas.
  • Spot vague answers and gently help the team get specific with the "ask why twice" habit.
  • Check that their "How might we" statement connects to what they learned in parts 01 to 04.
  • Return to their answers when they're stuck or choosing between ideas.
  • Let the team own and update their answers. Don't rewrite them.

Coaching questions

Ask before you answer

When a team is stuck, try one of these before offering an answer.

When they are…Try asking
Choosing an ideaWho are you making this for? What problem do they face today?
Choosing a formatWould a content strategy, a film or reel, or a game best carry this idea? What do you enjoy making most as a team?
Overwhelmed by scopeIf you could only finish one thing by tomorrow afternoon, what would it be?
Stuck on a problemWhat have you already tried? What is the smallest next step?
Disagreeing as a teamWhat do you each want the result to be? Where do you agree already?
Preparing to presentIn one sentence, what do you want people to remember?
Feeling discouragedWhat is going well so far? What would help you most right now?

Care

Spiritual care and prayer

  • Offer to pray with a team (in person, a voice room or chat) at natural moments: when they start, when they're stuck, and when they finish.
  • Keep prayers short, simple, and kind. Invite, never pressure. Some participants may be new to faith.
  • Listen for what God may be stirring in a participant, such as a gift, a calling, or a burden. Gently reflect it back.
  • Be honest about your own journey. Humble stories encourage young people more than polished advice.
  • If someone shares something heavy, listen well, thank them for trusting you, and let the organizers know so they can follow up.

Safety and boundaries

  • Stay visible. Mentor in public channels and open voice rooms. Avoid private one-on-one DMs or calls with participants. If a private chat is needed, bring in a second mentor or an organizer.
  • Remind teams to drink water, eat, take short breaks, and rest.
  • Respect privacy. Don't screenshot, record, or post about participants without permission.
  • Keep all communication with participants appropriate and inside the event's Discord server.
  • If you see anything unsafe, unkind, or concerning, tell an organizer right away.

Judging

What teams are scored on

Mentors are also the judges. Presentations run 2:00–4:00 PM Saturday. Coach toward these criteria during the sprint, then score with them. If you mentored a team closely, skip scoring that team.

  1. Understanding of Nonoy20PTS

    Specific, real details, not generic ones. The idea grows out of the team's answers about his world, his heart, and his struggles.

    Top 16–20 · Mid 10–15 · Low 0–9
  2. Bridging the gap25PTS

    Speaks to what stands between Nonoy's curiosity and the good news. Clear, faithful, wise about Jesus. Invites conversation, never pressures.

    Top 20–25 · Mid 13–19 · Low 0–12
  3. Creativity and craft20PTS

    A fresh idea, well made for 24 hours. Polished enough to show what it would become.

    Top 16–20 · Mid 10–15 · Low 0–9
  4. Right format for Nonoy15PTS

    The content strategy, film or reel, or game fits where and when Nonoy is online. He could find it, finish it, and share it with a friend.

    Top 12–15 · Mid 8–11 · Low 0–7
  5. Teamwork and process10PTS

    Everyone contributed. The team used mentor feedback and worked well together on Discord.

    Top 8–10 · Mid 5–7 · Low 0–4
  6. Presentation10PTS

    Clear about who it's for, what they made, why, and what comes next. The "How might we" statement connects to the final work.

    Top 8–10 · Mid 5–7 · Low 0–4
  7. Total100PTS

How to score

  • Score on your own first, then compare with the other judges and average.
  • Judge what teams made in 24 hours, not how big their idea is.
  • Ties: the higher "Bridging the gap" score wins. If still tied, the higher "Understanding of Nonoy" score wins.
  • Use the printed scoring sheet or the online sheet below. Enter your scores in your own judge tab.
